# India OSINT

Public records, open data and tools for investigating stories in India. Every link was opened and checked on the date shown beside it.

**179 sources. Last full check: 2026-11-16.** 165 loaded for us; 14 could not be reached from our checker and are marked in the table. Many Indian government sites block visitors from outside India or time out under load, so a source marked that way may still work for you.

## How to read the tables

- **Access** says what stands between you and the data: *Open* (nothing), *Free login*, *Captcha* (free, but a captcha on each search), *Paid*, or *Partly paid*.
- **Run by** tells you whose data it is. A government portal, a company and a volunteer group each have different reasons to be right or wrong.
- **Checked** is the date we last opened the link. Portals move and die often. If the date is old, check the link before relying on it.
- Notes in *italics* are caveats we hit while testing.

## What is deliberately left out

This list covers records about companies, governments, courts, public spending, land, the environment and public infrastructure. It does not include services that look up a private person's phone number, address, identity documents or vehicle ownership, or anything built on leaked or breached data. Those are not open sources, and using them can put both you and the person at risk.

## Using public records well

- **A record shows what was filed, not what is true.** Company filings, affidavits and tender documents are claims made by the filer.
- **Archive what you find.** Government pages change without notice. Save the page to the Wayback Machine and keep a copy with the date.
- **Names are not unique.** Match on a second detail (an identification number, an address, a date) before saying two records are the same person or company.
- **Check the law before you scrape.** Many portals forbid automated access in their terms, and some data, though visible, is covered by privacy law.

## Contributing

Corrections matter more than additions. If a link has moved, a portal has started charging, or a description is wrong, please open an issue or a pull request.

To add a source, add an object to `research/entries.json` with these fields and run `python3 build_readme.py`:

```json
{
  "category": "Courts and law",
  "name": "Name of the source",
  "url": "https://...",
  "what": "One sentence on what a journalist can find there.",
  "access": "open | free login | captcha | paid | partly paid",
  "run_by": "Who runs it",
  "status": "ok | unreachable | changed",
  "checked": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "note": "Optional caveat"
}
```

Please open the link yourself on the day you add it, and keep to the scope above: no people-search services and nothing built on leaked data.
